# NVIDIA: Revisiting the AI Boom and the Investment Risk

**Core concept**: The long-term promise of AI and the investment return of any single company are two different questions. An industry can keep growing without its leader keeping its share, pricing power, and excess profits forever. Capital spending must ultimately be carried by real commercial returns, moats are dynamic, and leadership and governance are part of a company's value.

**Sources**: The Kobeissi Letter, "-$6 Trillion In 45 Days: Is This A 'Bubble' Or An Opportunity?", and Drew Cohen's long-form deep dive on NVIDIA (Nov 20-21, 2026). Discussed by Belinda with Lao Jia (ChatGPT), Xiao De (Claude), and Xiao Miu; the note was drafted mainly by Lao Jia and organized and archived by Xiao Miu.

## 1. What Each Article Actually Says

### The Kobeissi Letter: the macro and industry view

Markets are repricing the sustainability of the AI investment boom. Enormous capital expenditures, financing structures, the interest-rate environment, corporate free cash flow, and the commercial returns on AI could all trigger the next leg of adjustment.

The core question: **can investment at this scale ultimately convert into sufficient economic return?**

Kobeissi's take (their view, recorded as such, not as fact): this is not a bubble popping but a normal correction. Markets have already priced in a pessimistic script — higher-for-longer rates, aggressive regulation, capital spending that never pays back, turmoil at the leading companies — so risk-reward is improving. But they do not claim the bottom is in.

### Drew Cohen: the company and competition view

NVIDIA still has strong operating results, profitability, and system-level competitive advantages — but its moat is evolving.

CUDA's edge is gradually being challenged. Google's TPU, AMD, and the hyperscalers' in-house chips are closing the gap. NVIDIA's core strength is shifting from a single GPU to the coordination of chips, networking, software, and complete AI systems.

At the same time, the leadership and governance risk from Jensen Huang's departure, together with circular financing across the supply chain, adds uncertainty about what comes next.

The core question: **even if the AI industry keeps growing, can NVIDIA hold its share, pricing power, and excess profits?**

Drew's landing point: he refuses to offer a precise valuation. Confidence in the people running the company is exactly what is missing right now — and that discomfort may be precisely why the opportunity, if there is one, exists.

## 2. The Investment Logic Both Articles Reveal

1. **A technological revolution does not mean every participant earns an investment return.** AI's long-term outlook and today's market valuations are separate questions.
2. **Industry growth does not mean the leader keeps excess profits forever.** Competition, customer bargaining power, and technological substitution keep redistributing the profit pool.
3. **Capital spending must ultimately be supported by real commercial returns.** If the economic value AI applications create cannot match the enormous investment, the industry can go through cyclical overbuilding and correction.
4. **Moats are dynamic.** NVIDIA has to keep innovating to hold its system-level performance edge, its share, and its pricing power.
5. **Leadership and governance are part of enterprise value.** After a founder leaves, whether the organization can keep executing the original strategy is a variable worth watching for a long time.

---

## 3. The Investment Judgment

We do not dismiss AI's long-term trend, nor NVIDIA's industry position. But we stay cautious about its future growth rate, margins, and valuation.

For investors and would-be investors in the current situation, doing nothing is itself a legitimate decision. There is no need to rush to buy the dip or add to a position. What is worth watching:

* Whether the new leadership can stabilize the team and carry the technology strategy forward.
* Whether competitors genuinely erode NVIDIA's system-level advantage.
* Whether the large cloud providers' AI capital spending can continue.
* Whether AI commercialization revenue gradually catches up with infrastructure investment.
* Whether NVIDIA can sustain high gross margins and free cash flow.
* Near-term checkpoints: the December 1 congressional hearing, and the appointment of a permanent CEO.

---

## 4. Investment Principles

Price determines when to watch; fundamentals determine whether to add.

A falling stock price does not automatically mean cheap, and short-term uncertainty does not erase long-term value.

Do not rush to call the bottom, do not bet heavily on a single scenario, and keep both capital and judgment in reserve.

---

## Final Takeaway

AI may be a technological revolution that runs for decades, but the companies within it — the competitive landscape, the distribution of profits, and the investment returns — can all change dramatically along the way.

Markets cannot predict the future accurately, and investors do not need to pretend they can.

Views can be clear while conclusions keep their margin. Actions can be decisive while position sizes stay disciplined.

The goal is not to be right every time, but to be able to respond sensibly under different futures.

**Believe in the long-term progress of the technology — but do not bet too heavily on any single company's long-term victory.**
