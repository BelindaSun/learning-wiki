# Domain Expertise and Organizational Change in the Agent Era

> **Core idea:** Agents commoditize execution faster than judgment. The scarce skill becomes knowing what matters, what good looks like, and what can go wrong.

## Why experts often gain more leverage

AI helps beginners, but delegation is different from tutoring. A domain expert can specify constraints, recognize weak output, and intervene at the right moment.

A novice asks, “Design a building.” An architect specifies structure, loads, climate, codes, and failure conditions. The Agent is the same; the executable information is not.

Expertise is therefore being reordered:

- **know-what:** facts—more accessible;
- **know-how:** routine execution—more automatable;
- **know-why:** explanation—partly accessible;
- **know-what-matters:** priority and framing—more valuable;
- **know-what-good-looks-like:** evaluation—more valuable;
- **know-what-can-go-wrong:** risk intuition—more valuable.

## Human planning, Agent execution

This is a useful default, not a permanent law. Humans define intent, constraints, and quality; Agents perform bounded work and return evidence. As trust improves, the boundary can move—but judgment must remain somewhere in the system.

## Individual productivity is not organizational productivity

An employee may become faster while the company gains little because approval queues, incentives, data access, and team handoffs remain unchanged.

```text
individual leverage
× process redesign
× shared infrastructure
× incentives and adoption
= organizational leverage
```

Installing an Agent without redesigning the workflow often accelerates one step and leaves the real bottleneck untouched.

## Four levels of managing Agents

1. **Instruction:** express a bounded task clearly.
2. **Evaluation:** judge whether the result is good enough.
3. **Orchestration:** decompose, route, and recombine work.
4. **System design:** build data, permissions, feedback, and incentives around the workflow.

The higher levels depend increasingly on domain and organizational knowledge.

## What happens when one person manages ten Agents?

Management span may widen, but coordination does not disappear. Organizations may need fewer layers devoted to information relay and more roles devoted to setting direction, resolving conflicts, and owning outcomes.

Junior development becomes a serious design problem. If Agents absorb routine tasks, novices may lose the practice through which experts once formed. Organizations must deliberately create supervised learning loops, not assume expertise will replenish itself.

## The durable principle

Domain expertise is not a pile of facts competing with a model. It is a compressed model of consequence: which variables matter, where reality resists the plan, and when an apparently good answer is unsafe.
