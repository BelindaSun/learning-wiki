# Google’s AI Reorganization: Designing for Two Time Horizons

> **Core idea:** The important change is not a title. It is the separation of long-horizon scientific ambition from medium-horizon execution.

The emerging division can be understood as:

- **Demis Hassabis:** AGI, scientific discovery, and long-term research.
- **Koray Kavukcuoglu:** models, research execution, Gemini, and developer products.
- **Sundar Pichai:** company-wide resource allocation and commercial strategy.

## Why separate time horizons?

Research and product execution optimize for different clocks. Frontier science needs room for uncertain bets. Products need deadlines, integration, reliability, and feedback from users.

Combining both under one leader can create a permanent conflict: urgent delivery consumes long-term attention, while scientific ambition delays operational decisions. Separation helps only if the two tracks still exchange knowledge and share priorities.

## AI competition is a system

Google's advantage cannot be evaluated from model rankings alone. The full chain is:

```text
science → compute → models → engineering → products
        → distribution → revenue → reinvestment
```

Google owns important assets across that chain. Its historical question has been whether research strength converts into coherent products and market advantage quickly enough.

## Ceiling × ability to reach it

A useful company model is:

```text
future competitiveness = technical ceiling × organizational ability to reach it
```

Hassabis may influence the ceiling. Kavukcuoglu may influence the throughput that turns research into deployed capability. Either factor near zero limits the result.

## Risks to watch

- coordination between long- and short-horizon groups;
- duplication or unclear decision rights;
- loss of top researchers or product leaders;
- product fragmentation across Google's distribution surfaces;
- pressure for quarterly delivery crowding out scientific bets.

## A broader organizational lesson

The best person should not necessarily own the most responsibilities. Good design gives exceptional people the problems for which their judgment is unusually valuable, then builds an interface to the rest of the organization.

This article interprets a changing organization from public information. Titles and reporting lines can change; the durable model is the separation and reconnection of time horizons.
