# Skills and the Business Landscape

> **The central question:** If reusable AI instructions are easy to copy, where does durable business value remain?

## First: what is a Skill?

A **Skill** packages a reusable way of doing a task: instructions, steps, rules, examples, and sometimes scripts or tool usage.

Without one, the user repeatedly explains the same preferences. With one, the method becomes reusable. But a Skill usually does not contain the customer's private data, privileged system access, legal accountability, or an ongoing service relationship.

## Three layers of value

### Model companies sell intelligence

They provide increasingly general reasoning and generation. As general capability improves, simple task wrappers become easier to reproduce.

### Integrators sell trust and relationships

They connect models to real organizations: data, permissions, workflows, support, compliance, and responsibility when something fails.

### Individuals and small teams capture the gap

When users can write or install their own Skills, they can remove an intermediary from tasks whose workflow is understandable and whose execution risk is low.

“Transparent” does not mean the middle layer disappears. It means the user no longer needs to think about it.

## Vertical vs general Agents

The difference is not simply capability.

- A **general Agent** can reason across many domains and use many tools.
- A **vertical Agent** owns a specific workflow, data relationship, risk model, and service experience.

As general models improve, a vertical product cannot rely only on a better prompt. Its defensibility comes from what surrounds the prompt.

## A Skill is an entry point, not a moat

Textual procedures are cheap to inspect, imitate, and improve. Durable advantage may instead come from:

- proprietary or permissioned data;
- accumulated user context and feedback loops;
- trusted integrations and distribution;
- domain-specific evaluation;
- compliance, support, and accountability;
- a service that reliably completes the whole job.

Consider a translation Skill. Its style rules can be copied. A translation service may still be valuable if it manages terminology across years, routes sensitive documents securely, supports reviewers, guarantees turnaround, and accepts responsibility for delivery.

## What is likely to weaken

Products whose whole value is “send a generic prompt to a model and display the answer” face pressure. When the underlying model exposes the same capability directly, the wrapper has little leverage.

Products are more durable when they own an outcome rather than an interface: they understand the customer, connect to the necessary systems, and handle the unglamorous exceptions.

## The practical test

Ask four questions:

1. If the Skill text leaked tomorrow, what value would remain?
2. What does the product know that a general model does not?
3. What real-world access or trust has been earned?
4. Who handles failure, ambiguity, and accountability?

If the answers are “nothing,” “nothing,” “none,” and “the user,” the business is probably a thin wrapper.
